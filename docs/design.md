# Design and page behaviour

See [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md) for data rules and commands, and [operations](operations.md) for publishing and deployment. This document describes the shared UI and the behaviour to preserve when changing it.

## Routes and shared shell

The front page is the finder, with one address per tab: `/` All, `/dining`, `/cafes`, `/bars`, `/formal`. `SCOPES` in `src/lib/finder.ts` defines their headings, labels and meta descriptions. Headings use the words people search for (“Cambridge college bars”); the tabs' titles and descriptions add “map”, “canteens”, “opening times” and “today”, and the front page's description says “Cambridge college grub”. The description is metadata only: under the heading the list starts straight away. The WebSite and Organization structured data carry `SITE_ALTERNATE_NAMES` (“grub”, “Cambridge grub”) for search engines' site names. A site has `/:slug`; a venue has `/:site/:venue`. Other pages are `/send`, `/coverage`, `/about`, `/terms`, `/credits` and a real 404.

Keep old links working: `legacyPath()` handles `/explore?…`, `/?type=…` and `/?venue=<id>`; `/directory`, `/colleges` and `/university` redirect to `/` in the app and `public/_redirects`. Old site-page `#venue-slug` links redirect to the venue. There is no separate directory or site index: find a site through search or `SitePicker`.

The header is shared across pages: name, BETA label, search (only a search: the finder's heading switches between tabs), Contribute (an AddCircle pill in the action colour, the one thing asked of every visitor; the icon alone on a phone), theme toggle and account control. Those three share `.header-pill`: as tall as the search, edged as it is (the `--edge` hairline, round Contribute's fill too, and a faint shadow), their words and icons as large as its text (`text-lg`, 24px icons). The name sits up a tenth of its size, so its mostly lowercase letters, and the Beta pill beside them, are level with the search. GitHub, with its star count, is on Contribute and in the footer. The mark is an ermine fork (`src/lib/mark.ts`): three tines over the tail of the ermine spot from the University's arms, the outer tines curling out as the tail's outer prongs do, every tip and notch rounded alike. The header draws it before the name in the action colour (`Mark`), and the footer before the ©; the favicon is it in Cambridge Blue on a Dark Blue tile. `npm run favicon` writes `public/favicon.svg` from the mark, then its PNG, ICO, Apple touch icon, the 512px `logo.png` named as the Organization's logo in the structured data, and `og.png` (1200 × 630, the mark and name drawn by headless Chrome in the site's fonts), every page's link preview unless it has a photo of its own. Never draw from the arms themselves (lions, cross and book): they are the University's. GitHub stars are read at build time in `vite.config.ts` and omitted when unavailable. The account control depends on the session and whether the sign-in provider is enabled. Contribution links belong beside gaps, in the footer and in the header's Contribute.

At 1280px and wider the header is one row, with search centred in three columns. Below that, search has its own row. On a phone's finder it sits over the map or at the top of the list instead. Search filters as you type; elsewhere it opens `/?q=…`. There is no search button. The header stays in view above phone width, and sticky content clears its measured `--header-h`.

The footer has About, Find and Help links (Contribute, sending in, coverage, issues, GitHub), coverage counts, copyright, terms and the not-affiliated line. No page has a back button or breadcrumbs in the visible UI. `useScrollMemory` restores scroll per history entry, and opens a new page at the part its `#hash` names.

## Finder

Each tab's heading (`h1`, read out, in the words people search for: the home tab's “Cambridge college and University menus, cafés and bars”, Dining's “Cambridge college menus”) is shown as the switch between tabs (`ScopeSwitch`), with the icons the header search's tabs had: “The University of Cambridge’s” (“Cambridge’s” while the list column is under 41.25rem), then on the same line a tab each (Food & drink, Dining halls, Cafés, Bars, Formals), the open one its icon and name, set to its left, in a Cambridge Blue pill always as wide as the longest name's, so the tabs never move, the others their icon alone, each a link to its own address keeping the shared filters. It is sized in its own em with its font from the list's width (`cqi`), the tabs at its right edge, so with the longest name open (“Food & drink”) it spans the cards exactly; `--k-short` and `--k-full` are that line's width in em, measured, to update if the names or sizes change. The search's placeholder names what it searches, formally: “Search the University of Cambridge’s dining halls, cafés and bars…”, and each tab's own. The phone’s map keeps its full screen. Footer links use descriptive category headings.

From tablet width up, the finder is a list of `VenueRow`s beside a square map, as tall as the screen allows under the header (at most 55% of the width, square still). The list starts with the tab's heading, then its filters as chips in one row, each with its icon, the count at its right end (staying at the top of the list as it scrolls; on a phone, under the tabs): Hours (Clock: Open now first, as Google Maps', then on Dining which day's menus, “Today” by default), the colleges and sites (`SitePicker`, MapPoint, “Anywhere” until some are picked; sites with nothing in the tab, and a kind with none, such as University sites under Formal halls, are greyed), on Dining alone, whose menus they are of, the meal (Plate) and the diet (Leaf); then, once anything is picked, Clear (a round ✕ chip, named on hover). Every chip opens the same kind of list (`ChipSelect`, ours, not the platform's select): under the chip (on a phone, the screen's width, still under it: `useDrop()`); then 24 rows at a time (`PAGE`) and “Show 24 more”, and at the list's foot the coverage line (`tabCoverage()`). Filters reset the count; a pin can reveal enough rows to reach its venue. Prerendered HTML includes every row. Rows are in the order of their status lines (`applyFilters()`, by `Ranked.shown`): open first, those with something to show (a menu, posted prices, formal hall's facts) before those only asking for it, each lot closing soonest first but those closing within half an hour (`CLOSING_SOON`) last; then by when each opens for the meal its row shows; ties by type (Dining, Cafés, Bars), something to show, how many dishes the row lists (the next menu's, never a meal already served), then site. A search ranks by relevance first. The list scrolls independently without a scrollbar; the map clears the header and window edge by the page gutter. After the list ends, the page can scroll to the footer.

Rows have a photo at least 4:3, up to 15rem wide, 20px corners, running the row's full height so nothing hangs below it (on a phone a 4.5rem thumbnail beside the name, the menu full width under them, so it stays readable); its map pin's mark in the top-left corner (`PinMark`, larger: its type's colour and glyph, split for two types) on every tab and page, with no word, so the row reads as its pin's key. Beside it, the site's short name (“Robinson”, as on map pins, in Dark Cherry) and the venue's name share one heading line, a muted dot between them (“Robinson · Garden Restaurant”); then one status line, a little smaller (meal, next service today, with its end when it starts before the one on now ends: “Open until 22:00 · Bar 17:00–00:00”, distance when available); then the detail, a little larger, so the menu leads. It fills at most four lines, a heading among them, so with the photo at its full size the list ends with it (1.125rem, 0.25rem apart), and a laptop's list shows three and a half to four rows; the last dish also says how many more there are (“Plant Based Lincolnshire Sausage +17 more”). The detail under them depends on the tab:

- Dining: the menu of the service the status points at (`nextService()`, `Ranked.next`): tomorrow's once today's meals are over, never a meal already served, else the next one with dishes that day. A row names one meal: while the venue is closed or yet to open, its status line is for the meal whose menu it lists (not breakfast over lunch's dishes; `menuStatus()`), and heads the dishes by name, so they read as that menu: “Tomorrow's lunch menu · 12:20–13:45”, “Today's dinner menu · ● until 19:30” (`menuName()`), formal hall by when it starts (“Tomorrow's formal menu · 19:30”). A meal with no hours of its own, served within a café's (Scholars Brew's lunch), is headed with the café's: “Today's lunch menu · ● until 17:00”. Open now for another meal, the status says so, and the dishes are headed in the same words (“Today's lunch menu · 12:20–13:45” under “Breakfast until 09:30”, the status then dropping its own “· Lunch 12:20”). Rows without a listed menu, and venue pages, keep the shared status wording. Main dishes, a bullet each; `mainDishes()` prefers `course: main`, else all dishes. Each dish carries one `Code`, its strongest meat-free one (`plantTag()`: VG, else PB, else V), so a vegetarian can run down the list; other codes stay on the venue's page. Without a menu: headline prices (at a meal, only what's sold at it), else “No dinner menu yet” linked to `/send` for that date and meal (“posted”, without a link, for a scripted menu). Venues used only for formal hall are excluded from Dining; on All they give formal hall's facts, as under Formal hall, unless their menu is up. On the Dining tab, venues without a menu for the date and meal picked are folded into one line at the foot of the list (`NoMenuLine`: “No menu yet at 16 more: …”, each linked to its page and lighting its pin, a generic name after its site's, “Clare Buttery”, then “Send one in”), unless a search or a diet is set; they stay on the map and in the count.
- Cafés and Bars: hours and usual prices from `headlinePrices()` (coffee/tea/food, or beer/wine/spirit/soft drink), each price near its name in a column at most 20rem wide, and two columns (up to eight) where the row has room. Dining without dishes can also use headline course or meal prices.
- Formal hall: When, Price, Dress and Booking using `src/lib/formal.ts`. Under the tab's list, “Compare every formal hall” (`FormalCompare`, folded) sets every one side by side, cheapest first.
- Search: what matched, as dishes, with their codes. Gaps: all in Dark Crest (`--alert`), one colour: a note icon in its disc (`--alert-bg`, 56px), what is missing, and under it the link to `/send` for it. A dining hall asks for today's or tomorrow's menu by name; one further off (Wednesday's, on a Sunday) isn't anyone's to send yet, so it asks for none in particular.

Picking a desktop pin reduces the list to that venue under “Show all N venues”. That link or a map-background click restores the full list and its scroll position. No duplicate card floats over the desktop map. Hovering a row highlights its pin. The initial fit uses the central cluster (`core`, within 1.5 km of Cambridge's middle); narrowing the list fits every remaining venue.

On a phone the finder opens on the list; “Map” switches to `view=map`, where the map fills the screen below the measured `--finder-top` under the same bar. The heading is available to screen readers. One line, edge to edge, heads both (`.finder-bar`, staying at the top as the list scrolls): the search as a short box, its magnifying glass and “Search…” (tapped, the field fills the line with the keyboard up and ✕ to close it; a typed search keeps it open), then the five tabs packed beside it, as a laptop's (the open one named in its pill, the others their icon alone). Under it the filters, as a laptop's row of chips, scrolling sideways, the count at its end; over the map they float on its top, without the count (its List button says). On the map, a selected pin shows swipeable `MapCards` at the foot. Search selects its first result, clearing it hides the cards, and the map follows the selected card. Cards sit above the keyboard using `--keyboard` from the visual viewport. “List · N” goes back to the list; old `view=list` links still open it. Only mount a map where one is shown.

Filters are validated by `readFilters()`. Shared across tabs: `q`, comma-separated `site`, `kind=college|university` and `open`. Dining also has `date` within the loaded week, `meal` and comma-separated `diet`. `SitePicker` has All / Colleges / University, search and any number of site checkboxes; on phones it rises from the foot. Nearest first is the map's own locate button beside a list (`.finder-locate`, top-left, Cambridge Blue while on), and on a phone a chip among the filters after Hours, on the list and the map alike. Where the browser would ask, it's also offered unasked (`LocateOffer`: “See what's nearest you” and ✕, which puts it away for good in this browser): on the map beside its button, and on a phone where a note goes, over the list or the map. Where the browser already allows location (`navigator.permissions`), it's found as the page opens, with no prompt, unless nearest first was last turned off here (`useHere()`). It asks only on a click otherwise, tries a quick rough fix then GPS, says which settings to check on this device (iPhone, Android, Mac) when it fails, keeps location in memory, sorts by straight-line distance (`metresBetween()`) and shows that distance. Walking directions open in Apple Maps or Google Maps (`directions()`), each pill with its app's icon (`MapAppIcons.tsx`): both under a venue page's map and beside a picked pin's row, and a round button on a phone's map card, for the device's own maps. Once location is known, the map draws the walk to the venue picked (a pin or row clicked, a phone's card swiped to) and to a venue page's venue, blue under the pins (in the theme's blue), the map fitted in one move to the walk, the venue and you, never closer than zoom 17, its time in place of the row's distance ("13 min walk") and beside Directions; never on hover. Routes come from OpenStreetMap's foot routing (FOSSGIS's OSRM, `lib/route.ts`), asked once per venue and kept for the visit; a venue page that doesn't know where you are offers "Show the walk from here". Location found on one page is kept for the others. A picked pin near the map's edge (or, on a phone, near the card) is moved to the middle of the space left free, unless its walk is on the way; Apple's map loses a move asked for while another is under way, so each waits for the one before to end (`whenStill()`).

## Site and venue pages

A site's and a venue's page have no lead paragraph: the name, its type labels and the facts say what it is, and the words people search with (“lunch menu”, “formal hall”, “opening times”) go in the title and description (`src/lib/seo.ts`, `src/lib/summary.ts`). Verified aliases from Supabase aren't shown on the page: a site's first alias is in its titles (“St Edmund's College (Eddies)”), and every alias is in the description. Spelling variants already covered by a name are omitted. The same names are included in descriptions and schema.org `alternateName`; site aliases also appear in page titles. No nickname is invented in code.

Both use a banner across the page (a venue without a photo gets its type's tile as a short band instead), with "Have a better photo?" in its top corner (a member's photo, once checked, replaces it, credited over its foot), then a main column and a square map in a rail. A site's main column has its name, venue count, website and `VenueRow`s without the repeated site label, ordered Dining → Cafés → Bars. Its map fits the site's venues (`maxZoom` 17); a pin marks and scrolls to its row.

A venue page keeps to the essentials: what's served, when and where, and what's missing. It starts with its site in Dark Cherry over its name, then types and formal hall (bow-tie mark) when applicable. Confirmed entry and guest requirements (`access_note`) appear in the details above the menu, with its one source link and “Report a change”; diets are not summarised there, the codes' key under the menu says them. Its rail has status and walk-in hours (only a link to send them when none are known), a menu calendar once there are menu dates, then “Missing here” in red (`HelpCard`), then a square map centred on the venue and its location text. “Missing here” is the one place for what's missing beyond what the page shows (a Dining venue's prices, formal hall's price or time, a photo, diets); the page itself says what's missing only where the gap is all there'd be: a day with no menu, a meal with no dishes, a café's or bar's empty list. Reviews are off (`REVIEWS` in `src/lib/site.ts`): no section, no ratings on the page, its rows or in structured data; what's written is kept. The map includes the site's other venues and opens their pages on click. The rail sticks only when it fits (`useFits`); it never scrolls independently. On phones, hours come before menu sections, then details, what's missing and the map.

Hours show walk-in times for the current term/vacation period, falling back to the other period when only that is known. A venue serving more than one meal gets a row per meal (`mealHours()`): its name, each time, and that time's days (“Except Sat” within the table), with the days under the time when the rail is narrower than 17rem. A single-service venue groups days with identical times (`weekHours()`). What's served today is in ink, the rest muted. The table is the usual week: a change posted in today's menu notes is an amber “Today:” line under it, and `withDayTimes()` applies it to opening status everywhere (rows, map cards, the venue's status line). `formatDays()` writes one missing day as “Daily except Sat” or “Mon–Fri except Wed”. Formal hall has its own section.

Sections follow the venue's types:

- Dining shows a dated menu, with date controls and history from `useMenuDates()` / `useMenuOn()`. The date opens a calendar on phones. Meals appear one under another, headed by name and hours; there are no meal tabs. Viewing today, meals whose every sitting has ended fold to their heading (“Served earlier”), so the current or next meal is first in view; once all are over, none fold. A day's note giving a meal other times (“Dinner 18:00–18:45 this Monday”) heads that meal with them in amber (`--notice`), with the note and the usual times under it. A note marked as an alert (`menu_days.alert`: a disruption that day, such as a meal moved after a leak) gets the same box in red (`--alert`) with an alert icon; other notes (“Pie night”) stay small and muted. Course and section headings are smaller than dish names, in the venue type's colour (`.type-ink` under the section's `data-type`); diet codes are small tags (`Code`), Green-tinted for vegan, plant-based and vegetarian and grey for the rest; a dish's allergens, where its source lists them, are one small muted line under it (“Contains celery, gluten (wheat) · may contain nuts”, `allergenText()`), with no warning on each menu (About says allergens are the venue's to confirm); prices align at the right. Each price is said once, at the most general level it's true: a meal's in the meal heading, a course's (its list price, or one every dish in it shares) in the course heading, else each dish's on its row (`coursePrice()`). A meal with no dated dishes but its own posted list can show that list. A venue with no dated menu history can show its posted prices instead.
- Formal hall follows Dining, or comes first when it is all the venue holds: when, prices, guests, dress and booking, including unknown fields.
- Café and Bar show undated posted lists. `priceGroups()` splits lines by the services they belong to, puts course/meal prices beside dishes or in headings, and folds other items under “Deals and extras”. The first type's section is “Menu”; later ones use their type name. A café or bar with dated dishes also gets “Daily menu”. A single posted section, or a list with item descriptions, uses the full column width; multiple short sections can flow into two columns. Descriptions and choices sit beneath the dish name in smaller, muted text, with prices aligned beside the name.

Missing menus and price lists use `GapCard`: Light Blue, brief explanation, photo or typed text accepted, one pill action. A Dining menu without a price list says so once, in a Light Blue `GapLine` at the top of the menu; today's menu on a day without one has an inline contribution link. `venueGaps()` drives both the rail's help and Contribute; the rail's help leaves out what the page already asks for where it would be (the menu, a café or bar list, hours), and adds unknown diets except for bars.

An explicit dated meal cancellation (`No dinner – …` in the menu note, with no dishes) stays in the calendar and menu. Its heading says “Not served”, without the usual hours or prices; the note gives the published reason. Opening status excludes that meal on that date, and the hours rail includes the notice when it applies today. The usual weekly hours stay visible. A missing menu alone never means a cancelled service.

A meal whose menu is posted elsewhere (a note with no dishes that isn't a cancellation: “European Night, see posters for details”) keeps its usual hours, shows its note in place of dishes, and from yesterday on has a gap line to send a photo of it (`/send` with that date and meal). A venue row whose current meal is one shows its note under the meal's name.

A café or bar's dated schedule note can give a one-off time range in the same way, without changing its usual weekly hours. A café with a bar service in the loaded dated schedule appears under both types, as it does with recurring bar hours. Formal notes that publish a start alone (“Starts 19:00”) show that dated start in the formal details without inventing a finishing time.

Reviews use `Reviews.tsx`: anonymous ratings after five members, newest six written reviews then “Show all”, and the member's own form when signed in. Signed out, the prompt to rate is a line with a secondary pill, quieter than the page's gaps. `RatingSummary` appears beside venue names; SEO includes `aggregateRating` when available.

## Contribute and information pages

`/contribute` (formerly `/coverage`, which redirects with its filters) is where filling grub.cam in lives, kept lean: the title, then "Send what you see" as a card in the gap style (Light Blue, the camera in a disc, what happens to it, an arrow; the whole card the link), so it isn't the header's pill again. Two columns from a laptop (3fr, 2fr): the card then Coverage on the left; on the right, from the card's top, the leaderboard, Just added and **Open source** (the rarer way to help, so last: the GitHub pill, `GitHubPill`, with a star and the count, beside its heading, and a line on helping with the menu scripts). On a phone: the card, the right column, then Coverage. **Coverage** (`#coverage`, a neutral heading: it opens on the most complete) is one list: the colleges, or University sites (`Segmented`), each a row with its place, name, a meter and how many things are missing (Dark Cherry, `--site-ink`), which opens onto its venues and their gaps, each a link to the relevant `/send` form. `siteProgress()` weighs what their venues could have (`venueNeeds()`, by `gapWeight()`); "What" narrows it to one kind (menus, prices, hours, formal hall, photos), counted instead ("2/3"), and "Order" is most or least complete or A–Z. Level shares share a place. Ten rows, then "Show all". The URL keeps `kind`, `missing`, `order` and the open colleges (`site`); a link with `site` opens and scrolls to it. `venueGaps()` never asks members for a scripted menu. **Top contributors** (all time, the first three in gold, silver and bronze; "How points work" folded) and **Just added** (five) read the public `contributions` view in the browser only (`useContributions()`). Points (`scored()` in `src/lib/contributors.ts`) are `gapWeight()`: 4 for a dining hall's menu, 3 for a café's or bar's price list or any venue's hours, 1 for anything else; only the first of a kind for a venue on a London day (a menu: its date and meal) scores, whoever sent it.

Price lists keep a posted “N/A” or “TBC” in the appropriate second price column, alongside numeric prices. These labels are not item descriptions, and an empty cell stays empty.

What members send goes up under their name (`submissions.credit`, the name their Cambridge account gives, else their CRSid), so a wrong menu has someone behind it. A venue page says who: "Prices as posted …, sent in by …" in the price list's note, "Sent in by …" under the hours, "This menu was sent in by …" under a dated menu (`venue_credits`, loaded with the week; `useMenuCredits()` for other dates); a menu not sent in says where the venue posts it instead ("Menu from chu.cam.ac.uk ↗", `venues.menu_url`). Photos keep their banner credit.

Each link off the site appears once on a page, in words saying what it is (`venueLinks()`, `src/lib/links.ts`). Under a venue's name: its own page ("More at …"), else the page its menu comes from ("Menu from catering.emma.cam.ac.uk ↗"), unless that's a file (a PDF), which stays under the menu. The college's general page is on the college's page only ("More at …"), never repeated on its venues'. Under the menu, formal hall ("More on formal hall at …") and the price list ("Prices as posted … at …"), a source is linked only when nothing above already is. A meal's note that only gives its usual times, as the heading already does, isn't shown.

`/send` is one centred column, as wide as the header's search (46rem), and one step. It names the venue (a card with "Change", or a search by venue, site or alias), or with `?site=` a college or site itself, whose page's banner a photo is for (no kind chips then: a site takes only its photo), what it is (icon chips: Menu, Prices, Hours, Venue photo, Correction), then the files: "Take a photo" (touch screens) or "Upload", photos or PDFs, up to ten, as thumbnails, with a context box beside them ("next week's menu"), which takes the typed text when there's no photo. "Send" (Sparkles, for what a model reads) shows the photos swept by a band of light and the reading arriving a line at a time, with the seconds counting; then "It's going up" (sent in by whom) with links to the venue and to send another, and to the leaderboard, or the model's reason over the form, files kept, to try again. A correction's box is required and goes to a person. Under the button: "Goes up as <name> (<address>)". On a phone the button stays at the foot of the screen. Copy is short: one line under the title.

About explains the site, live coverage, ways to help and who makes it. Terms covers use, privacy (including the cookieless Cloudflare Web Analytics beacon, which `vite.config.ts` adds to built pages only), allergies, no affiliation and services that see visitors' IPs. Credits lists site and venue photos with author, licence and source.

## Visual conventions

`html { font-size: 90% }` scales rem sizes, including Tailwind. Quoted pixel sizes in comments assume 16px per rem; media-query rem breakpoints use the browser's default size. Use rem for layout; reserve px for hairlines, rings, shadows and map marks sized against the basemap.

`Container` aligns header and footer with 16/24/48px gutters and a 120rem maximum width. The finder uses those outer edges; other pages have a centred 76rem column, with a 22rem rail on large screens (17rem at tablet width). Lists and dated meals stay one column. Contribute's two columns, fixed price-list sections and reviews can use multiple columns.

Use Figtree for titles and venue names, Public Sans for body and controls, self-hosted through fontsource. Both are variable, set lighter than usual so pages feel delicate: text 350, medium 450, semibold 550 and bold (titles) 600, from the `--font-weight-*` theme tokens and `body`. Tokens in `index.css` use the University palette: Slate text, Cambridge Blue for selection, Green dot plus Dark Green “Open”, Dark Cherry site names (`--site-ink`), Cambridge Dark Blue for the primary action/focus, Warm Blue link underlines, and deeper Dark Blue backgrounds in dark mode. The site uses neither University branding marks nor its typeface.

Controls share heights through `Controls.tsx` and `.btn`, `.chip`, `.field`, `.icon-btn`. Actions and filter chips are pills; ordinary controls use 8px corners and panels 12px. Avoid boxes inside boxes, tracked uppercase labels, arrows on buttons, and a hairline border plus wide shadow on the same box (`.raised` chooses one).

Icons come through `components/Icon.tsx` and `lib/icons.ts`: ForkKnife, HotDrink (cup and saucer), Wineglass2, custom BowTie. `TypeLabel` keeps names, glyphs and pin colours consistent. Type colours are Dining Crest, Café Green and Bar Indigo; these are the deliberate exception to avoiding indigo elsewhere. Status wording comes from `statusWords()`: “Open until …”, “Opens tomorrow …”; a venue serving more than one meal leads with the meal instead (“Dinner until 19:30”, “Breakfast tomorrow 07:30”), as formal hall always does, while café and bar hours stay “Open until 20:00” even at a café-bar (`leadsWithMeal()`) (“Formal hall 19:30”, never “opens” for a booked formal). Open is a Green dot then the whole line in Dark Green, its first word semibold; screen readers hear “Open:” before a meal's name. The theme toggle shows the current theme (sun in light, moon in dark).

## Maps

`VenueMap` uses Apple MapKit JS when `VITE_MAPKIT_TOKEN` works, otherwise OpenFreeMap through dynamically imported MapLibre GL. Shared props, marks and framing live in `MapParts.tsx` and `mapView.ts`. Apple uses Standard style and `PointOfInterestFilter.excludingAllCategories` (the runtime name, unlike the typings' `filterExcludingAllCategories`), so its own points do not compete with venues. OpenFreeMap uses Bright / Fiord with attribution. Theme changes preserve camera and selection.

Each venue has one pin; never cluster them. `sideBySide()` offsets venues sharing a point or within 8 m (pair, triangle, then grid). Pins are 24px discs with white glyph/rim, sheen and shadow; dual-type venues split colours diagonally. Open status belongs in the list, not the pin. Selection is larger and ringed. Pin and user-dot origins must have zero size: MapKit measures rendered annotation size, so a sized origin can make marks drift.

At `LABEL_ZOOM` 15, and always for selection, `pinLabel()` adds a name with site context where needed, leaving the site out on an embedded map whose pins are all one site's. `placeLabels()` / `labelPins()` place it right or left without overlap. Preserve `labelUntilMeasured()`: MapKit reports movement before drawing, so labels need repeated measurement until projection and rendered widths agree.

Finder maps accept wheel and pinch with rotation disabled. Embedded site/venue maps let the wheel scroll the page. Desktop Safari pinch handling is in `mapGestures.ts` and bound only to the map; browser gestures elsewhere stay intact. Phone finder maps hide zoom buttons. Nearest first adds the user dot; standalone maps can have Near me and Show all controls.

`REACH` bounds zoom-out to the venue area plus about 600 m (Madingley Hall to the Biomedical Campus, Girton to Addenbrooke's). Minimum zoom depends on map size; MapKit uses the equivalent camera height and limits its centre. Widen it for a venue beyond those bounds. Finder fitting stops at zoom 15; site maps at 17; venue maps focus at street zoom.
